# Layered Coherence

*A framework for finding out what is, and a starting model of reality built with it.*

---

## In brief

Everything known is known through records: traces the past leaves in the present. Layered Coherence turns records into knowledge by stacking claims in layers, each answerable to the layers beneath it, with confidence highest at the base and earned at every step above.

**The framework**

- Build from records upward. Each layer constrains the ones above it.
- Confidence comes from distance to records and tests survived, never from appeal.
- When a claim fails, trace the failure down to the link that broke and fix the narrowest thing first.

**The starting model**

- The universe makes records; records make learning possible; learning makes correction possible.
- Persistent systems capture energy gradients to maintain, repair, and copy themselves; agents model the world to steer that process.
- Agents build environments that then select and shape the agents. Worldviews are the models that steer this loop.

## Part 1 — The framework

### 1.1 The engine

Finding out is one loop:

```
record → model → prediction → comparison → correction → better model
```

A record is a trace of what happened. A model compresses records. A prediction is the model's bet about records not yet seen. Comparison exposes the error, and correction shrinks it. Science, history, engineering, auditing, and a person learning from a mistake all run this same loop. A claim that cannot enter it is not knowledge.

### 1.2 The structure

Reality is modeled as a stack of layers. Each layer is made of the one below plus new organization, and each lower layer supplies the priors that higher layers must fit.

- **Constraint runs up.** A higher-layer claim must fit the priors beneath it.
- **Correction runs down.** When a higher-layer claim keeps failing, a bridge or a prior below it is wrong.
- **Confidence tapers.** The base has the most records and the simplest structure. Each layer up adds degrees of freedom and loses clean tests.
- **Higher layers are real.** Organisms, institutions, and technologies are new structure that becomes new constraint. Layering is not reduction: each layer answers its own questions with its own evidence. It cannot contradict the layers beneath it or pose as one of them.

Seen from inside, the stack is a flashlight: a narrow, bright center of direct record, and a beam that reaches farther and grows dimmer as it widens.

### 1.3 The unit

Every claim has three parts:

- **Claim:** the statement, placed on a layer.
- **Priors:** the lower-layer facts it rests on.
- **Bridge:** the assumption connecting the claim to its priors.

Errors hide in bridges. The claim card makes them visible:

```
CLAIM       one sentence
LAYER       where it sits in the stack
PRIORS      the lower-layer facts it needs
BRIDGE      the assumption linking them       ← check here first
WRONG IF    the observation that would refute it
RISKS       how it fails or gets misused
TEST        the smallest unit that can check it
STATUS      rung on the ladder + confidence
```

### 1.4 Three rules

1. **Traceable down.** Every claim names its priors and its bridge.
2. **Correctable up.** No prior is protected from failed prediction. Revise in order: measurement, then claim, then bridge, then prior.
3. **Earned confidence.** Confidence is set by layer position and tests survived. Elegance, authority, and popularity add nothing.

> No claim without a bridge. No bridge without priors. No prior protected from failed prediction.

### 1.5 The cycle

| Move | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Locate** | Place the claim on a layer. Name its priors and its bridge. |
| **Expose** | State what would refute it and how it could fail or be misused. |
| **Test** | Run the smallest real test. Record the expectation before the outcome. |
| **Update** | Fix the narrowest broken part. Promote only after repeated survival. |

### 1.6 The ladder

```
conjecture → hypothesis → tested → established → foundational
```

A claim becomes a prior for other claims only once it is established. Foundational claims carry the most weight, so they face the hardest tests. The ladder blocks two failures: building on what hasn't been tested, and testing forever without ever building on what has survived.

### 1.7 Failure modes

| Too loose | Too rigid |
|---|---|
| **Floating claim:** no priors underneath | **Protected prior:** a base assumption shielded from refutation |
| **Overconfident bridge:** a weak link treated as strong | **Flattening:** denying a higher layer is real because it is made of a lower one |
| **Shortcut:** a conclusion asserted instead of traced | |
| **Aesthetic capture:** keeping a claim because it is satisfying | |
| **Proxy capture:** improving the measure instead of the thing measured | |

## Part 2 — The starting model

### 2.1 The stack

```
 7  Culture    norms · practices · institutions · symbols · worldviews   lowest
 6  Society    cooperation · trust · coordination · transmission
 5  Niche      tools · built environments · technologies · markets
 4  Human      embodied · social · language-using · model-building
 3  Agency     sensing · acting · feedback · models · affect
 2  Life       metabolism · repair · reproduction · heredity · selection
 1  Physics    time arrow · records · entropy · gradients · constraints  highest
                                                                   confidence
```

### 2.2 Layer by layer

**1. Physics.** The past is low-entropy, and coarse-graining turns that into a time arrow. The arrow is what makes records: the past leaves traces, the future does not. Causality follows from the arrow, which is why we can learn from the past and act on the future and never the reverse. Gradients make work possible; constraints channel it.

**2. Life.** Life is organized matter that persists by capturing gradients and exporting entropy. A living system has a boundary, maintains itself, repairs damage, and reproduces. Heredity plus variation makes lineages, and selection is the accounting of which forms persisted.

**3. Agency.** Agency is viability control: sensing, acting, and using feedback to keep the coupling between self and world within livable bounds. Intelligence is control mediated by models. Affect is the agent's readout of its own state: evidence, and trainable toward accuracy.

**4. Human.** Humans are embodied, social, model-building agents. Language and records let models outlive the people who made them. Human representations come in two kinds: those that refer to the world, which are checked against the world, and those that refer to other representations, which are checked against people. Both are real, and confusing one for the other is the most common source of disagreement. Meaning is integrated agency: model, narrative, goal, role, and feedback cohering into one direction.

**5. Niche.** Agents modify their environments, and modified environments select and train the agents who live in them. Every tool, institution, and technology is a selection environment. Building moves faster than the slower systems that regulate it, so technology repeatedly reshapes people before people reshape their rules.

**6. Society.** Trust is scoped, stake-weighted reliance: on a specific party, for a specific matter, backed by evidence and consequence. Cooperation holds when repeated interaction, identifiable actors, visible conduct, memory, credible commitment, and exit make contributing pay better than defecting. When a signal becomes cheap to fake, trust moves to costly, witnessed proof.

**7. Culture.** Norms are control policies a group uses to steer conduct. Religions, philosophies, ideologies, and sciences are all world-models for action: compressions of reality into guidance for attention, decision, and transmission. They differ in validation discipline, not in kind. A worldview persists only as a transmission package, bundled with livelihood, kinship, status, ritual, governance, child-rearing, apprenticeship, and records. Belief without the bundle ends with its founders.

### 2.3 The loop through the stack

```
     models → action → practice → inherited form
       ↑                                  ↓
better models ← future agents ← selection ← built environment
```

Builders are built by what they build. Models guide action, action shapes environments, environments select the next generation of agents, and those agents build the next models.

### 2.4 The model in nine laws

1. Records make correction possible.
2. Gradients make work possible; constraints channel it.
3. Selection keeps what persists, not what is true or intended.
4. Builders are built by what they build.
5. Trust is scoped reliance backed by consequence.
6. Cheap signals push trust toward costly proof.
7. A measure detaches from what it measures once it becomes a target.
8. Systems either renew the sources they draw on or consume them. Only the first compounds.
9. Models persist only when carried by the practices that transmit them.

---

## Part 3 — How the framework and the model fit

The framework is the model's first law made deliberate. The universe makes records, life uses them, agents model them, and Layered Coherence is agents doing this on purpose at every layer, including the top layer where their own worldviews live.

The framework also applies to itself. Every claim in the starting model sits on the ladder and goes through the cycle like any other. The starting model is where finding out begins, and the framework is how it keeps going.
